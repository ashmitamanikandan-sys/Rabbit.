## What is RABBIT?

**RABBIT** is a book-discovery web app prototype built around a simple idea: **what if discovering a great book felt as effortless as scrolling your favorite social feed?**

Instead of judging a book by its cover or reading a lengthy synopsis, RABBIT gives you a compelling idea from inside it. Explore bite-sized, original chapter-inspired insights, discover unexpected perspectives, and follow your curiosity down a new rabbit hole.

## The experience

- **Choose your interests.** Start with *The Human Mind*; more topics are planned.
- **Discover by scrolling.** A vertical, one-post-at-a-time feed introduces ideas from different books and chapters.
- **Read a little, think a lot.** Each post offers a concise, curiosity-provoking insight; **Read More** opens a richer reading experience.
- **Follow books.** Book-cover story circles surface chapters from books you follow.
- **Make it yours.** Like and save insights, with preferences remembered locally in your browser.
- **Explore further.** Find other chapters and related books from the reading view.

## Design philosophy

**Dark, immersive, and deliberately minimal.** RABBIT pairs a pitch-black interface with restrained midnight-lilac details, editorial reading typography, real book-cover imagery, and the original RABBIT wordmark with lilac rabbit-ear double Bs and a neatly placed lilac full stop. The goal is to make books feel at home in a modern discovery feed without losing the pleasure of reading.

## Built with

- HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript
- Browser `localStorage` for likes, saves, and followed books
- A self-contained `index.html` prototype, with embedded cover imagery
- GitHub Pages for static hosting

No account or installation is required. Open the link above in a modern browser.

## Prototype status

RABBIT is an **early interactive prototype**, not a finished social platform. *The Human Mind* is the active interest category; other interests, account features, and social interactions are planned for future development. Some social controls are demonstrations rather than connected services.
